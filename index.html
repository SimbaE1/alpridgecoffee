Alpine Ridge is a plan for a very small coffee shop on the coldest continent: eighteen seats, one roaster, and a window that faces nothing but ice. It does not exist. Everything below is what it would be if someone built it.

The Room

Simple, because everything else there isn't.

The design is deliberately plain: plywood, wool, brass, and one long bar. No music, no pastry-case theatre, no menu board that takes five minutes to read. A counter, a kettle, and someone with time to talk to you — which at that latitude would be the actual luxury.

The sketch puts the building on stilts so drift passes underneath, with the lamps left on through the polar night. That's the part we're most confident about and the part that would cost the most.

  • Room for eighteen. Standing room for a few more.
  • Boots off at the door; wool socks by the bench.
  • Designed to run through the dark months, if it ever runs at all.

Sourcing

"Locally sourced," with an asterisk

Nothing agricultural grows within thousands of kilometres of the site, so the word local has to be used carefully. The aim is a short, boring supply chain and the shortest possible last mile — not a claim we can't keep.

Green coffee

Would arrive by seasonal resupply, twice a year, and be stored cold. The cold storage is the one part that costs nothing.

Roasted on site

A single 5 kg drum roaster in the back room. Small batches, because the whole operation would be a small batch.

Water

Melted from ice cut nearby. This is the only genuinely local ingredient, and probably the best thing about the idea.

Goods

Mugs, caps and knitwear made by whoever is wintering over. Stock would be whatever actually got finished.
