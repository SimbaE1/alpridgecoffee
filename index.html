The Room

Simple, because everything else there isn't.

The room is deliberately plain: plywood, wool, brass, and one long bar. No music, no pastry-case theatre, no menu board that takes five minutes to read. A counter, a kettle, and someone with time to talk to you — which at that latitude would be the actual luxury.

In the drawings the building sits on stilts so drift passes underneath, and the lamps stay on through the polar night. It is a nice thing to picture. That is as far as it goes.

  • Room for eighteen. Standing room for a few more.
  • Boots off at the door; wool socks by the bench.
  • Lit through the dark months, in the version that exists on paper.

Sourcing

"Locally sourced," with an asterisk

Nothing agricultural grows within thousands of kilometres of the place, so the word local has to be used carefully — even in an imaginary shop. The idea is a short, boring supply chain and the shortest possible last mile.

Green coffee

Would arrive by seasonal resupply, twice a year, and be stored cold. The cold storage is the one part that costs nothing.

Roasted on site

A single 5 kg drum roaster in the back room. Small batches, because the whole operation would be a small batch.

Water

Melted from ice cut nearby. This is the only genuinely local ingredient, and probably the best thing about the idea.

Goods

Mugs, caps and knitwear made by whoever is wintering over. Stock would be whatever actually got finished.
